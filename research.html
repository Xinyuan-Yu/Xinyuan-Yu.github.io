Research

I am interested in how short-lived climate forcers shape air quality, climate, and human health. My earlier work at Peking University modeled black carbon and fine particulate matter and the health benefits of cleaner energy and air. At MIT, I focus on tropospheric ozone — both an air pollutant and a greenhouse gas — and ask what has driven its changes and how they affect Earth's climate.

Ozone & climate

MIT · 2022–present
Detection & attributionTropospheric ozone trends

A human fingerprint in rising upper tropospheric ozone

Using a chemistry–climate model ensemble and a 17-year satellite record, I show that the increase in northern midlatitude upper tropospheric ozone reflects human activity rather than climate noise.

Radiative forcingOzone precursors

What drove 1995–2014 ozone increases, and what it means for forcing

Sensitivity simulations attribute recent tropospheric ozone increases to surface anthropogenic NOx emissions and quantify the resulting ozone and methane radiative forcing.

Air quality & health

Peking University · with Prof. Shu Tao · 2016–2022

My work in Prof. Shu Tao's group followed pollution from its sources to its consequences: building emission inventories, modeling how those emissions shape air quality, and estimating the health impacts of policies that reduce them.
