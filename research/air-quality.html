Drivers and health impacts of PM2.5 pollution

Policies → Emissions Meteorology ↘ ↗ PM2.5 → Human health

Separating the effects of emissions and weather conditions. Understanding how emissions and weather conditions shape fine particulate matter (PM2.5) pollution is essential for evaluating air-quality policies. We combine chemical transport modeling with statistical methods to distinguish their contributions, with a particular focus on black carbon. We developed direct and inverse reduced-form models to estimate black carbon concentrations from emissions and infer emissions from concentrations while accounting for meteorological influences (Yu et al., 2021). Related work showed that substantial PM2.5 reductions across Chinese cities persisted after accounting for favorable weather conditions, providing evidence of the effectiveness of emission controls (Zhong et al., 2021).

Health benefits of cleaner energy and air-quality policies. We update the PKU emission inventory, developed by the Tao Group and collaborators, and combine it with air-quality modeling, population exposure estimates, and health-impact assessment to quantify how changes in energy use and policy affect human health. Our studies examine cleaner household energy, targeted clean-heating strategies, and more stringent regional air-quality standards (Zhang et al., 2021; Meng et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2023). These studies highlight the importance of where and how fuels are used: residential coal combustion contributes disproportionately to PM2.5 exposure and associated mortality, making household energy transitions an important opportunity for improving public health (Yun et al., 2021).
