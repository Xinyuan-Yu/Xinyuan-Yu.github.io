Surface NOx emissions drove 1995–2014 increases in tropospheric ozone

Tropospheric ozone kept rising between 1995 and 2014 even as air quality improved in many regions. I use targeted chemistry–climate simulations to attribute this increase to individual precursors and regions, and to quantify the radiative forcing it produced — for ozone itself and, through changes in the hydroxyl radical, for methane.

Approach

Starting from the 1995–2014 historical simulation, I perturbed one driver at a time — global and Asian surface anthropogenic emissions, NOx and non-methane VOCs separately, and the methane concentration — to see how much each contributed to the change in tropospheric ozone and to its radiative forcing.

What we found

  • Surface anthropogenic NOx emissions are the main driver of the 1995–2014 increase in tropospheric ozone, with Asian emissions playing a large role.
  • The same NOx emissions boost the hydroxyl radical and shorten methane's lifetime, so part of their positive ozone forcing is offset by a negative methane forcing.
  • Rising methane adds to ozone forcing directly, so precursor-specific accounting matters for climate policy.

Related directions

With collaborators, I also estimate the ozone forcing that US air pollution controls have avoided. My ongoing work extends these questions to the climate response to preindustrial-to-present-day ozone changes.
